# Animal research

Real-world numbers for candidate species, and what each could mean for the sim. Figures vary a lot by region and source; ranges are what the sources report.

## Current species

**Eastern cottontail.** 3 to 4 litters a year (up to 7 in the south), 3 to 8 young each. Most die within a year. Home range 5 to 8 acres. Mammals (coyotes, bobcats, foxes) cause about 55% of deaths, hawks and owls about 25%. [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Sylvilagus_floridanus/), [Georgia DNR](https://georgiawildlife.com/sites/default/files/wrd/pdf/fact-sheets/Rabbit%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf), [MDC](https://mdc.mo.gov/index%2ephp/node/218991)

**Red fox.** Omnivore: mostly rodents and rabbits, plus insects and fruit. Home range 5 to 12 km² in good habitat, up to 50 in poor; territory shrinks when prey is dense. Litters average 4 to 6. Wild lifespan 1 to 5 years. [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Vulpes_vulpes), [Rutgers](https://njaes.rutgers.edu/FS1325), [Mammal Society](https://mammal.org.uk/british-mammals/red-fox)

## Candidates

**Meadow vole.** Extreme breeder: females mature at 28 days, 4 to 8 litters a year of 4 to 6 young. Average lifespan under a month (most die as juveniles); max 16 months. Populations peak every 2 to 5 years. A staple food for foxes, coyotes, hawks, and owls. [ESF](https://www.esf.edu/aec/adks/mammals/meadow_vole.php), [ADF&G](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=meadowvole.main), [USFS FEIS](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/mipe)

**Red-tailed hawk.** 80 to 85% small rodents, some rabbits. Hunts from a high perch over open ground, usually at forest edges. Home range 1.3 to 5.2 km². [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Buteo_jamaicensis/), [Audubon](https://www.audubon.org/birds/red-tailed-hawk), [Maryland DNR](https://dnr.maryland.gov/wildlife/Pages/plants_wildlife/Red-tailed_Hawk.aspx)

**White-tailed deer.** Herbivore, eats whatever vegetation is around, shifting by season. Does range about 1 mi², bucks about 2.5. One fawn as a yearling, usually twins after; fewer in lean years. Wild lifespan 4 to 10 years. Coyotes take fawns. [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Odocoileus_virginianus/), [Maine IFW](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/fish-wildlife/wildlife/species-information/mammals/deer.html), [NC State](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/white-tailed-deer)

**Coyote.** Opportunist: in one study 33% rabbits, 25% carrion, 18% rodents. Switches to fawns in late spring; much deer in its diet is scavenged. Packs of a breeding pair plus older offspring who help raise pups. Litters about 6, smaller when coyotes are dense (4.3 vs 6.9). Range about 10 to 20 km². [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/site/accounts/information/Canis_latrans.html), [Texas Tech](https://www.depts.ttu.edu/nsrl/mammals-of-texas-online-edition/Accounts_Carnivora/Canis_latrans.php), [NCpedia](https://ncpedia.org/node/478)

**Coyotes vs foxes.** Coyotes dominate and sometimes kill red foxes (intraguild predation). Foxes cope by avoiding coyotes in space and time, not by strict territories. The effect varies by region; some studies find none. [Newsome & Ripple 2015](https://thomasnewsome.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/newsome_ripple_journal-of-animal-ecology_2015.pdf), [PMC9251843](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9251843)

## A benchmark: snowshoe hare and lynx

The classic real cycle. Lynx peaks follow hare peaks by 1 to 3 years, and the cycle repeats every 8 to 11 years. Hares keep cycling even with lynx removed or extra food, so food and climate matter too, not just predators. Our rabbit and fox peaks should show the same lag. [Krebs et al.](https://www.zoology.ubc.ca/~krebs/papers/180.pdf), [NWT](https://www.gov.nt.ca/ecc/en/services/lynx/lynx-snowshoe-hare-cycle)

## What it means for the sim

- **Foxes should eat voles too.** Real foxes eat mostly rodents. A predator that switches to whatever prey is plentiful damps crashes ("prey switching" is a known stabilizer), so voles could make the whole system steadier.
- **Hawks fit the forest rule.** They hunt open ground from forest edges, so forest cover protects prey from hawks even more than from foxes. Flying, they ignore water.
- **Prey size should set the reward.** Today a fox's meal size is a fox stat. With several prey, it belongs to the prey: a vole is a snack, a rabbit a meal, a fawn a feast.
- **Coyotes add a second layer.** They hunt the same prey as foxes and occasionally foxes themselves, so foxes should flee coyotes.
- **Deer compete with rabbits for food** and are slow breeders, so they'd drift on a slower cycle than rabbits.
